Why berth at Riverboat?

A marina built to slow you down.

Walk to the boatyard and have your boat looked after by professionals. Walk the grounds for nothing but the pleasure of it. Or stay a while and become part of the family here. That has been the idea since the beginning.

The Delta's fresh water is good for boating all year, and with more than a thousand miles of connected waterways there is always somewhere new to run.

  • A well-kept marina with mature trees around the berths
  • Quick access to fast water and the San Joaquin River
  • Herons, egrets, hawks and owls in and around the marina
  • Delta Boat Works, a full-service boatyard, inside the gate
  • The Wheelhouse club for berthers, for gatherings up to 25
  • BBQ and picnic areas, and kayaks for tenants to use

The grounds

Twenty-six acres, and the herons were here first.

A horseshoe island of huge cottonwoods holds one of the largest great blue heron rookeries around, with more than a hundred nests. Across the water on Andrus Island, woods of about nineteen tree species shelter hawks, owls and songbirds.

Our history

The last Delta riverboat.

John Moore spent much of the Second World War as a shipwright at Mare Island. He loved two things: building and repairing boats, and playing his trumpet the way a big-band professional would.

He wanted a marina of his own, in the middle of the Delta where the boating was, with slack current, a site out of the wind, and a natural setting where people from the city could simply get away.

“A visit to Riverboat Marina will show that he accomplished his goals.”From the marina's own history
  1. Late 1950s

    The boatyard

    John begins Delta Boat Works and builds two boat elevator lifts.

  2. A first

    Covered berths

    He builds the first covered berths in California, just as he had pictured them.

  3. 1961

    The Sutter

    John buys the Sutter, the last used Delta riverboat, and spends five years converting her single-handed into Moore's Riverboat, a restaurant and bar known across the Delta.

  4. 1966

    A $250 handshake

    The Riverboat opens. John leases the boatyard to two Mare Island friends, Julio and Silvio Focaracci, on one sheet of paper at $250 a month. Seventeen years later the rent was still $250.

  5. 1984

    A new family

    John and the Focaraccis retire, and Ken Scheidegger and his family take over Riverboat Marina and Delta Boat Works, determined to carry the tradition on.

  6. 1991

    The yard grows

    Two large metal buildings, a 10,000-square-foot concrete work area, and a travel lift rated for 35 tons.
